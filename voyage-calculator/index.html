Dry Bulk Voyage Estimator

A full voyage estimation calculation for dry bulk: enter the route, vessel and freight rate and get voyage profit/loss, daily TCE, the breakeven freight rate and a cost breakdown. Leave the freight rate blank to get breakeven and costs only. Estimates you can override throughout.

Estimate only, from your inputs and representative defaults. Not a firm quote or chartering advice.

What a voyage estimation tells you

A voyage estimation answers one question: does this cargo, on this ship, over this route, make money? It brings together the freight revenue and every voyage cost — bunker fuel, port disbursements, canal dues — across the days the voyage takes, and returns the voyage result, the daily TCE, and the breakeven freight rate. Chartering desks run dozens of these a day to rank competing cargoes before fixing.

How the estimate is built

  • Sea days = distance ÷ (speed × 24). Port days = load + discharge.
  • Bunker cost = fuel burn at the chosen speed (scaled by the cube law) × bunker price, plus port consumption.
  • Voyage costs = bunkers + port disbursements + canal dues.
  • Breakeven rate = voyage costs ÷ (cargo × (1 − commission)).
  • Voyage result = net freight revenue − voyage costs; TCE = result ÷ voyage days.

Worked example

A Panamax lifts ~72,000 t of grain from the US Gulf to Qingdao (~11,400 nm). With bunkers at $600/t the voyage costs land around $0.73m over ~40 days, for a breakeven of roughly $10/t. Fix the cargo at $52/t and the voyage returns a strong positive result and a TCE near $75,000/day. Enter it above and vary the rate to find where the voyage turns red.

Keep it current

Bunker prices and freight rates move every day, so a voyage estimate is a snapshot. Pair it with the live Baltic Dry Index and sub-indices to judge whether the market is carrying rates up or down before you commit.
