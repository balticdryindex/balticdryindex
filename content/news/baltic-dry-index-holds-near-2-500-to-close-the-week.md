The \*\*Baltic Dry Index (BDI)\*\* closed the week near the 2,500 level, little changed as the dry bulk market settled into a steadier rhythm after recent volatility.

The larger \*\*Capesize\*\* segment continued to take its direction from iron ore flows on the key Brazil–China and West Australia–China routes, where cargo demand has stayed firm enough to keep rates above breakeven for most modern tonnage.

In the mid-size classes, \*\*Panamax\*\* and \*\*Supramax\*\* earnings tracked grain and coal movements out of the Atlantic and Pacific basins, with tonnage supply broadly balanced against available cargoes.

At current levels, the index points to a dry bulk market in reasonable health — not the elevated readings seen at cyclical peaks, but comfortably clear of the lows that strain vessel economics.
